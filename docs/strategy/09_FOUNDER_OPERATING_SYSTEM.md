# GeoViz — Founder Operating System

Status: PERMANENT. This is David's personal operating manual, derived from the board-level strategic conclusions already reached. It does not get re-litigated quarter to quarter — it gets executed.

## Focus by Stage

- **Launch → $250K:** Sell, deliver, and QA personally. Do not delegate fulfillment until every failure mode has been felt firsthand. Commit to one vertical.
- **$250K → $1M:** Hire the first engineer to kill the fulfillment bottleneck. Personally run the first five agency relationships before handing the channel to a hire.
- **$1M → $5M:** Shift from doing sales to building the sales system. Own the category narrative and PR personally. Resist board/advisor pressure toward premature enterprise sales.
- **$5M → $10M:** Personally own the first published benchmark report. Personally recruit the Head of Partnerships — this hire determines plugin distribution success and should not be delegated to a generic recruiting process.
- **$10M → $25M:** Shift further from GTM into competitive and platform-risk monitoring (Google/OpenAI native tooling risk). Personally sponsor the first three enterprise logos.
- **$25M → $100M:** Protect scoring integrity and mission as the final arbiter against growth-at-any-cost pressure from investors, competitors, or your own team. Honestly assess whether your own role should shift from operator to strategic/board-level, and act on that assessment rather than defending the founder title for its own sake.

## What David Should Stop Doing (by stage)

- Stop personally fulfilling every audit once Stage 1 exit criteria are met.
- Stop personally closing every sale once the sales system exists (Stage 3).
- Stop being the default owner of every partnership conversation once a Head of Partnerships is hired and proven (Stage 4).
- Stop being the primary voice in every enterprise deal once the enterprise sales motion is validated (Stage 5).
- Stop making unilateral product calls without the governance board once the Benchmark Engine's methodology-change process is formalized (Stage 4+, see `06_BENCHMARK_ENGINE.md`).

## Hiring Priorities in Order (do not reorder without a stage-exit-criteria reason)

1. Fulfillment/ops contractor (Stage 1)
2. First full-time engineer (Stage 2)
3. First Customer Success hire (Stage 2)
4. VP Engineering + first data scientist (Stage 3)
5. First dedicated marketer + first agency AE (Stage 3)

7. CRO, enterprise AEs/SEs, security/compliance lead, VP Data/ML (Stage 5)
8. Data-licensing/BD lead, Chief Trust Officer, product-line GMs (Stage 6)

## Weekly CEO Dashboard

Every week, David reviews (not more, not less):
- ARR/MRR movement and NRR trend
- Pipeline health for the current stage's primary GTM motion (direct, agency, enterprise, licensing — whichever is live)
- Fulfillment/QA health (refund rate, correction frequency, delivery time)
- One data-moat metric appropriate to the stage (raw signal volume → cohort size → sampling panel cost-per-query → dataset size vs. named competitors, as the stages progress)
- Any trust-integrity flag: any instance where scoring, evidence standards, or an automated action was questioned internally — these are reviewed personally, every time, with no exceptions delegated.

## Quarterly Planning

Each quarter: confirm the company's current stage against `01_FIVE_YEAR_ROADMAP.md` exit criteria (not against calendar time or investor expectations), name the top 3 priorities that move the company toward the next stage's exit criteria, and explicitly name what will NOT be worked on this quarter — the STOP list is as important as the START list at every planning cycle.

## Decision Making

Run every non-trivial decision through the Decision Filters in `00_NORTH_STAR.md` in order: Trust → Category → Moat → Phase → Scope → Revenue. If a decision fails an early filter, do not let a strong revenue case downstream override it — the filters are ordered deliberately so that revenue is never the tiebreaker on a trust or category question.

## Leadership Evolution

David's job changes shape at every stage: operator (Stage 1) → team-builder (Stage 2–3) → category-definer and external voice (Stage 4–5) → steward of mission and trust (Stage 6+). Each transition requires deliberately stepping back from the previous stage's hands-on work, not just adding new responsibilities on top of old ones — failing to let go of an earlier stage's role is the most common way a founder becomes the bottleneck at the next stage.

## Founder Mistakes to Avoid

- Building monitoring, dashboards, or subscription infrastructure before Stage 1 proves repeat-purchase demand exists.
- Chasing enterprise revenue before the product can survive real enterprise procurement scrutiny (a CIO will say no, and that "no" is data, not a rejection to argue with).
- Retuning the scoring rubric under pressure from a single big customer or a competitive scare — any scoring change follows the isolate/name/validate process in `CLAUDE.md`, without exception, regardless of who is asking.
- Publishing a benchmark or index before the methodology has been stable long enough to survive scrutiny (see `06_BENCHMARK_ENGINE.md`) — a retracted or revised public number costs more credibility than waiting one more quarter to publish.
- Shipping any automated action against a customer's live site without an approval gate and rollback path, regardless of competitive pressure to "move faster than the audit-only competitors."
- Over-investing engineering time in report/PDF polish or feature parity chasing relative to the data-moat infrastructure that actually determines the company's ceiling.
- Refusing to delegate a function (sales, fulfillment, partnerships) past the point where personal involvement is still adding unique value rather than just personal comfort.
